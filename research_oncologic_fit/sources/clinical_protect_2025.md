# PRO-TECT randomized trial: patient-reported symptom monitoring

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12184200/
- Title: Symptom monitoring with electronic patient-reported outcomes during cancer treatment: final results of the PRO-TECT cluster-randomized trial.
- Publication: 2025, Nature Medicine; issue April 2025. Retrieved: 11 October 2026.
- Type: Original multicentre cluster-randomized trial, 52 US community oncology practices, 1,191 adults with metastatic cancer.
- Access: Full text retrieved by read-only fallback after gstack cookies-required response.
- Quote: “Severe or worsening symptoms generated alerts to the care team.”
- Claim: Weekly symptom reporting plus clinician alerts improved several patient-centred outcomes; mean emergency visits at 12 months were 1.02 versus 1.30. Overall survival did not differ (hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.83–1.17; P=0.86). Alerts went to responsible nurses; clinician responses were discretionary. Paper discusses dedicated staff time and integration as implementation needs.
- Product implication: Follow-up should have an accountable response loop, with patient-reported outcomes measured separately from survival. Software alerts alone are not the studied service.
- Credibility: High original experimental evidence. Recency: Recent publication, but enrollment ended March 2020. Bias/limitations: Baseline imbalances from cluster randomization, pandemic disruption, restricted languages and US metastatic outpatient setting; applicability to this hospital/product remains unproven.
