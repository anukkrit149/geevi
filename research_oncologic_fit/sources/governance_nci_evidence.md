- Type: primary institutional description of editorial methods; secondary synthesis when describing individual studies.
- Credibility: high for formal evidence methods. Editorial board is independent of NCI; this is not an NCI policy statement or clinical guideline.
- Recency: older framework, still live and regularly reviewed; do not label it a new 2026 study.
- Bias/transfer: clinical-trial hierarchy has limitations in rare diseases; page explicitly recognizes observational evidence may be the only practical information.

## Verified claims
Study design and endpoint strength are ranked separately. Expert opinion is D evidence, while randomized evidence can be stronger. Recommendations also require considering toxicity, confidence intervals, trial size, quality assurance and cost. Board members review recently published articles monthly; updates use consensus.

Quote (10 words): “Changes to the summaries are made through a consensus process”.

The page summarizes Soni et al. (JCO 2019): 350 observational treatment comparisons were matched with 121 randomized trials; 40% agreed on treatment effects. This is counterevidence to treating a local observational dashboard as proof of superior treatment. It does not mean observational data are useless or every modern causal study fails. The underlying PubMed page (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30897037/) was attempted but cookie-blocked; cite the NCI page for these figures, explicitly as its summary, rather than claiming direct full-study review.

## Design inference
An evidence lead should curate and propose changes; a department should review/adopt them. Capture citation, evidence level, applicability, uncertainties and approval history. Preserve protocol version against each patient decision; review exceptions; avoid automatic outcome-driven protocol promotion.
